# Exam tone and marks

A question on an exam is not a quiz prompt or a chat message. It follows fixed
conventions.

## Phrasing

- **Open with an imperative verb** matched to the Bloom level: Calculate, Determine,
  Derive, Show that, Sketch, Draw, State, Define, Explain, Compare, Evaluate, Design.
- Give the scenario, then the data, then the task — in that order. One situation per
  question.
- State every datum the student needs, with units. Do not make them hunt or assume.
- For a closed question there is exactly one defensible answer. For an open one, say
  what a full answer must contain.

## Marks

- Every question (and every sub-part) carries a mark allocation: `[5 marks]` or `(5)`.
- Marks scale with the work: roughly 1 mark per minute of expected effort, or proportional
  to the number of correct steps.
- Show the total. A multi-part question lists each part's marks; they sum to the total.

## Sub-parts

- Break a multi-step problem into `(a)`, `(b)`, `(c)`. Each part is self-contained and
  separately marked.
- Later parts may build on earlier ones; if so, a student who got (a) wrong can still
  attempt (b) with a stated assumed value ("using your answer to (a), or R = 4 Ω…").

## Standard instructions

Use the conventional phrases where they apply: "Show all working.", "Give your answer to
3 significant figures.", "State any assumptions.", "You may use the formula sheet.",
"Diagrams need not be to scale."

## What an exam question never has

No preamble, no learning-objective commentary, no "this question is worth attempting",
no encouragement, no answer hints embedded in the stem.
